# Deal threshold demoted to human view-time judgment

This ADR **supersedes ADR 0001** (hybrid deal threshold) and, where they conflict, the stored-threshold parts of **ADR 0002**. The fair-price mechanism of ADR 0011 — median of a trailing 90-day window, minimum five observations — stands unchanged. What changes is what we do with the number once we have it.

ADR 0001 bought its threshold machinery — global default, per-identity overrides, IQR-fenced volatility refinement, surge hysteresis — to solve a problem that turned out not to exist for us: flagging deals automatically. The stored threshold never earned its keep. Real usage showed the buyer does not consult a system verdict of `deal` / `no_deal`; they look at the margin on a card and decide in a glance, with context no stored percentage captures — how urgently they want the item, whether the seller is nearby, whether the fair price itself looks trustworthy at six observations or thirty. The threshold was a number we computed, stored, refined, argued with, and ultimately routed around. Meanwhile it created real costs: every identity edit could change it, every synthesis had to carry it, every rejected price observation distorted it, and every explanation of the app had to explain three layers of threshold that produce one boolean the user overrides with their eyes.

We decided to **delete the stored threshold entirely**. No global default, no per-identity override, no volatility refinement, no surge state, no settings table. What remains in deal math is the arithmetic that survives on evidence alone: `effective_price` (asking + shipping − flip value) and `margin_pct = (fair − effective) / fair × 100`. Both are computed at read time from the price-observation history; neither is persisted. The deal feed shows every identified listing with a computable fair price, sorted by margin, and stops there. **Deal judgment is a human judgment made at the view layer**, not a stored claim.

Rejections now feed that judgment directly instead of distorting it: a rejection deletes the rejected listing's price observations under the mapped identity and lands the reason verbatim in the identity's `Exclusions:` block, where it survives every description rewrite. Restoring a rejection drops the line and re-records the observation. With `POST /api/v1/identities/reresolve` (MCP `reresolve`), the user can also re-run resolution over stored listing text with the full identity list whenever the catalog outgrows the old verdicts.

Rejected: keeping the threshold as a pure display default (a number that is never stored still has to be agreed with the data, and that agreement is exactly the machinery we deleted); a server-side "suggested threshold" (same modeling layer, softer verb); freezing the old schema with the column nullable (dead columns are how schemas rot).

Known consequence: without the threshold there is no automatic `deal` verdict, so anything that consumed one (the daily sweep, chat advice) now reads raw margins and quotes them with the cold-start caveat where `fair_price` is null.
